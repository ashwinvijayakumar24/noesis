# Reference Critique of the Draft Review Article  
**Title (implied): Review of Processability of Titanium Alloys in Metal Additive Manufacturing**

---

## 1. Major Methodological and Conceptual Issues

### 1.1 Lack of Transparent Review Methodology (HIGH)

The manuscript repeatedly refers to itself as a *“systematic review”*:

> “While systematically reviewing the works published in the literature…”  
> “This research aims to provide a comprehensive overview of recent advances…”

However, **no review methodology is described**. Missing elements include:

- Search databases used (Scopus, Web of Science, etc.)
- Search keywords and Boolean logic
- Inclusion/exclusion criteria
- Time range justification (“since 2014” is stated but not defended)
- Screening process (PRISMA flow diagram absent)
- Quality assessment of included studies
- Risk-of-bias considerations

Without these, the article is **not a systematic review**, but a narrative literature survey. Claiming systematic rigor without methodology is a serious scholarly issue.

**Reviewer 2 comment:** “The authors repeatedly describe this as a systematic review but provide no reproducible search strategy or inclusion criteria. This must be corrected.”

---

### 1.2 No Clear Analytical Framework for “Processability” (HIGH)

The central theme is *processability*, yet this term is not rigorously defined.

You state:

> “focus on the processability of common titanium alloys produced by different metal AM methods.”

But processability could include:
- Relative density
- Cracking susceptibility
- Balling behavior
- Process window width
- Melt pool stability
- Parameter sensitivity
- Powder recyclability
- Surface roughness
- Defect tolerance

Currently, the manuscript:
- Lists alloy examples.
- Reports densities and strengths.
- Mentions processing windows.

But there is **no structured evaluation framework** or cross-comparison matrix.

A review article should:
- Define measurable indicators of processability.
- Compare alloys across standardized criteria.
- Provide synthesis tables.
- Identify trends and gaps.

Right now, the text reads as a sequence of alloy case summaries.

---

### 1.3 Weak and Unsubstantiated Novelty Claim (HIGH)

You claim:

> “the lack of a comprehensive review article covering all processability elements… is felt.”

This is a bold claim requiring citation support.

There are already numerous reviews on:
- Ti-6Al-4V in AM
- β-Ti alloys in AM
- AM process–microstructure–property relationships
- WAAM of titanium alloys
- PBF vs EBM titanium comparisons

The manuscript does not:
- Compare itself explicitly to prior reviews.
- Identify what prior reviews omitted.
- Clarify what new structure or dataset it introduces.

**Without explicit differentiation, novelty is unclear.**

---

### 1.4 Overemphasis on Listing Equipment Manufacturers (MEDIUM)

The manuscript lists commercial systems:

> “Arcam AB, EOS, Concept Laser GE, Renishaw…”

This reads more like a marketing inventory than a scholarly justification. It is unclear:

- How equipment brand differences were analytically evaluated.
- Whether machine architecture differences were compared.
- Whether parameter ranges differ systematically by manufacturer.

Simply listing manufacturers does not strengthen scientific rigor.

---

### 1.5 Selection Criteria for Alloys Are Not Scientifically Operationalized (MEDIUM)

You state:

> “the selection of titanium alloys was based on several key criteria…”

But:

- No explicit inclusion boundaries are defined.
- No justification for excluding other relevant alloys (e.g., Ti-17, Ti-6242, Ti-6246).
- No explanation of whether dual-phase stability diagrams were considered.
- No discussion of compositional design strategies for AM (e.g., crack-resistant alloy design).

The criteria are broad and subjective.

## 2. Structural and Organizational Problems

### 2.1 Corrupted Text and Formatting Errors (HIGH)

There are major formatting issues:

- Abrupt insertion of DOI mid-paragraph:
  > “DOI: 10.1002/adem.202301122The restricted number…”

- Sentence fragmentation:
  > “mold sands. [15,16] Moreover…”

- Missing transitions.
- “Additive ManufacturingAM technology” (missing space).
- “selfdivided”
- Typographic encoding issue:
  > “α þ β Ti alloys”

These significantly reduce readability and professionalism.

---

### 2.2 Redundancy and Repetition (MEDIUM)

The introduction repeatedly:

- Defines AM.
- States benefits of AM.
- Mentions sustainability.
- Notes Ti cost issues.

The first ~3–4 paragraphs could be reduced by 40–50% without losing content.

---

### 2.3 Weak Abstract-Level Framing (HIGH)

Although no formal abstract is included here, the introduction functions like one. It fails to clearly state:

- What specific research question is being answered.
- What new synthesis is provided.
- What conclusions are drawn.
- What quantitative comparisons are made.

It reads as descriptive rather than analytical.

---

### 2.4 Section Transition Problems (MEDIUM)

Example:

> “For further details, one may refer to Section 2…”

But Section 2 begins later without clear narrative linkage.

The flow from:
- AM generalities
→ Titanium metallurgy
→ Alloy examples
→ Method selection
→ AM process description

is not logically staged.

A better structure would be:

1. Scope and novelty
2. Review methodology
3. Processability framework definition
4. Process–alloy interaction
5. Comparative synthesis
6. Application mapping
7. Future directions

---

## 3. Citation and Literature Gaps

### 3.1 Lack of Foundational AM Reviews (MEDIUM)

Seminal AM reviews appear missing (unless hidden in references not shown):

- Gibson, Rosen & Stucker (Additive Manufacturing Technologies)
- Herzog et al. (Acta Materialia, 2016) – metals AM review
- DebRoy et al. (Progress in Materials Science, 2018)
- Frazier (J. Materials Engineering and Performance)
- King et al. (microstructure in AM metals)

These works are foundational and should anchor the review.

---

### 3.2 Limited Discussion of Phase Transformation Physics (MEDIUM)

For Ti alloys, key metallurgical phenomena are underdeveloped:

- Martensitic α′ formation in Ti-6Al-4V
- β transus relevance in PBF/EBM
- Partitioning kinetics in metastable β alloys
- Oxygen pickup effects in AM
- Columnar prior-β grain growth mechanisms

The manuscript cites mechanical properties but does not deeply engage with transformation theory.

---

### 3.3 Sustainability Claims Not Properly Supported (LOW–MEDIUM)

Statements like:

> “AM significantly saves raw materials and uses no tools, minimizing production costs and energy.”

This is oversimplified and controversial. AM can be:
- Energy intensive
- Powder wasteful
- Costly per part

A lifecycle assessment citation would be needed.

---

## 4. Writing and Clarity Issues

### 4.1 Grammar and Syntax (MEDIUM)

Examples:

- “as objected to subtractive manufacturing”
- “selfdivided”
- “prepositioned powders”
- “Experiencing very high cooling rates… results in…”

Frequent minor grammatical errors suggest insufficient proofreading.

---

### 4.2 Overlong Sentences (MEDIUM)

Example:

> “This research aims to provide a comprehensive overview…”

This 5–6 clause sentence could be broken into 2–3 clearer statements.

---

### 4.3 Imprecise Claims (LOW–MEDIUM)

> “L-PBF is particularly notable for its exceptional precision…”

Compared to what? At what scale? Quantitative backing is needed.

---

## 5. Scientific Depth Issues

### 5.1 No Quantitative Cross-Comparison Tables (HIGH)

A strong review would include:

- Density vs. energy density plots.
- Mechanical property ranges by process.
- Cooling rate comparisons.
- Process window width comparisons.
- Summary tables per alloy family.

Currently, the text is narrative and anecdotal.

---

### 5.2 No Critical Discussion of Conflicting Results (HIGH)

The review cites:

- Higher strength in AM vs conventional.
- Higher density under certain parameters.

But does not discuss:
- Variability across studies.
- Statistical spread.
- Reproducibility issues.
- Machine-to-machine differences.

A review must evaluate reliability, not just report peak values.

# Final Evaluation

The topic is highly relevant and timely. The manuscript demonstrates broad literature coverage and domain familiarity. However, in its current form, it is:

- **Descriptive rather than analytical**
- **Claiming systematic rigor without methodology**
- **Structurally inconsistent**
- **Insufficiently differentiated from existing reviews**

Substantial restructuring, methodological transparency, analytical synthesis, and language revision are required before the manuscript could meet the standards of a high-impact materials science journal.
