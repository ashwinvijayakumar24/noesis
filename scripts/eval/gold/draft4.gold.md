# Reference Critique of Draft Manuscript  
**Title (inferred):** Microplastics in Freshwater Ecosystems: Sources, Fate, Impacts, and Remedial Measures  
**Overall Assessment:** Narrative review with broad scope but substantial structural, methodological, and scholarly deficiencies. The manuscript currently does not meet the standards of a rigorous review article.

---

# 🔴 HIGH SEVERITY ISSUES

## 1. ❗ Lack of Review Methodology (Not a Systematic or Structured Review)

> “The scopus search of country-wise research publications on MPs in freshwater environments is shown in Fig. 1.”

- No description of:
  - Search strings
  - Inclusion/exclusion criteria
  - Time window
  - Database limitations
  - Screening process
  - PRISMA-style flow
- The claim of being a “holistic review” is unsupported by a transparent methodology.
- The Scopus analysis is mentioned but not described (no method, no interpretation framework).
  
**Impact:** Undermines credibility and reproducibility. Review articles require methodological transparency.

**Reviewer 2 would say:**  
> “This is not a systematic review. The authors provide no methodological framework for literature selection, raising concerns about selection bias.”

---

## 2. ❗ Overstated and Scientifically Inaccurate Claims

### a) Toxicity Overgeneralization
> “Virulent nature of plastics is undeniable as they are made up of toxic chemicals.”

- Scientifically inaccurate. Plastics are not inherently “virulent.”
- Toxicity depends on:
  - Polymer type
  - Additives
  - Weathering state
  - Adsorbed pollutants
  - Dose and exposure pathway

This statement is sensational and unscientific.

---

### b) Freshwater More Severe Than Marine
> “The MPs pollution in freshwater water systems is more severe than in marine systems [10] because freshwater is more easily accessible than marine water.”

- Illogical causal reasoning.
- Accessibility ≠ severity.
- No quantitative comparative synthesis provided.

---

### c) “None have provided a comprehensive discussion”
> “To the best of our knowledge, none of them have provided a comprehensive discussion…”

This is demonstrably false. Multiple high-impact reviews (e.g., Wagner & Lambert 2018; Eerkes-Medrano 2015; Li et al. 2018; Blettler et al. 2018; WHO 2019; UNEP reports) provide comprehensive freshwater reviews.

**Impact:** Overclaims novelty.

---

## 3. ❗ No Critical Synthesis — Merely Descriptive

The manuscript:
- Lists concentrations from different regions
- Reports polymer types
- Mentions technologies

But does NOT:
- Compare methodologies (e.g., FTIR vs Raman vs visual ID)
- Normalize concentration units (particles/kg vs particles/L)
- Discuss sampling biases (mesh size, digestion protocols)
- Address contamination controls
- Evaluate quality assurance/quality control (QA/QC)

Example:
> “MPs in the river sediment varies from 87 to 4000 particles/kg.”

No discussion of:
- Extraction density
- Polymer identification method
- Size detection limits

**This is aggregation, not synthesis.**

---

## 4. ❗ No Risk Assessment Framework

The paper repeatedly claims health risks:

> “Prolonged exposure to MPs can lead to long time health risks.”

But:
- No dose-response discussion
- No exposure pathways quantified
- No differentiation between hazard and risk
- No toxicokinetics
- No regulatory thresholds cited

WHO (2019, 2022 updates) reports insufficient evidence for health risk at current drinking water concentrations — not discussed.

---

## 5. ❗ Treatment Technologies Section is Technically Weak

Problems:

- Conflates removal of additives with removal of microplastic particles.
- AOPs degrade organics; they do not necessarily mineralize solid polymers efficiently.
- No energy cost, scalability, fouling, byproduct discussion.
- No comparison of removal efficiencies.
- No cost-benefit analysis.

Example:
> “AOPs have been used to eliminate MPs…”

Most studies are lab-scale and often degrade surface chemistry rather than fully mineralize polymers.

**Reviewer 2 would say:**  
> “The treatment section is overly optimistic and ignores scalability constraints.”

---

## 6. ❗ Missing Major Literature and Policy Context

Absent references/frameworks:
- WHO (2019) microplastics in drinking water
- UNEP 2021–2023 plastic pollution reports
- SAPEA 2019 evidence review
- GESAMP reports
- ISO standardization efforts
- Nanoplastics literature
- Atmospheric deposition literature (Dris et al.)
- Harmonized methods discussion (Koelmans et al.)

# 🟠 MEDIUM SEVERITY ISSUES

## 7. Weak Novelty Positioning

Claim:
> “This article presents a holistic review…”

But no conceptual model, framework, or new synthesis is offered.

To justify publication, the paper needs:
- A fate-transport conceptual diagram
- Comparative meta-analysis table
- Standardization critique
- Research gap matrix
- Policy roadmap

Currently, it reads like a graduate-level overview.

---

- No dedicated:
  - Abstract (in provided text)
  - Methodology section
  - Risk assessment section
  - Limitations section
  - Future research framework section

---

## 9. Inconsistent Units and Comparability Issues

Reported units:
- particles/kg
- particles/L
- metric tons
- %

No effort to standardize or interpret comparability.

---

## 10. Logical Inconsistencies

Example:
> “There is no significant relationship between concentration and population density…”

Immediately followed by:
> “Peng et al. identified concentration two magnitudes higher in urban area…”

Contradiction is not analyzed or reconciled.

# 🟡 LOW SEVERITY ISSUES

## 11. Writing and Grammar Problems

Frequent issues:

- “MPs contains” (should be contain)
- “one among them”
- “long time health risks”
- “virulent nature”
- “are ended up”
- “MP is distinguished… Because MP is more robust…”

Run-on sentences and inconsistent tense.

Tone is occasionally informal and alarmist.

---

## 12. Terminology Problems

- Uses “MP” and “MPs” inconsistently.
- Defines microplastics as “<5 mm to 1 μm” — excludes nanoplastics discussion.
- No clarification of lower detection limits.

---

## 13. Figures and Tables Not Properly Integrated

> “Table 1” and “Fig. 1” referenced but:
- No interpretation
- No synthesis discussion
- No statistical insight

# ✅ Recommendations for Major Revision

## 1. Add a Review Methodology Section
- Database(s)
- Search terms
- Inclusion/exclusion criteria
- Screening flow diagram

## 2. Provide Critical Comparative Tables
- Sampling methods vs concentration
- Detection methods vs size threshold
- Removal technologies vs efficiency vs scale

## 3. Add Risk Assessment Framework
- Hazard vs exposure vs risk
- Drinking water thresholds
- Food web transfer evidence

## 4. Reframe Novelty
Instead of “holistic review,” position as:
- “Critical appraisal of freshwater MP quantification challenges”
or
- “Methodological inconsistencies in freshwater MP studies”

## 5. Strengthen Treatment Section
- Removal efficiencies
- Scale (lab/pilot/full)
- Energy demand
- Cost
- Sludge disposal implications

## 6. Tone Down Overstatements
Replace:
- “Virulent nature is undeniable”
With:
- “Certain polymers and additives may pose toxicological concerns depending on exposure.”
