# Noesis Lab Outreach Plan — 5-Day Sprint
Last updated: May 10, 2026

---

## Strategic Foundation

### Core Positioning (Keep From prompt.md — This Is Right)
> "Noesis reviews a manuscript against the lab's own literature library and flags unsupported claims, missing citations, coverage gaps, and reviewer-style concerns before submission."
> "Reviewer 2 before Reviewer 2."

Do not pitch as AI writing tool. Do not pitch as general productivity tool.
Pitch as: **pre-submission peer review grounded in your own literature**.

### What This Sprint Is Actually For
Not signups. Not revenue. **Conversations.**
- Each call/email reply = one data point on willingness to pay and which workflow matters
- 10 good conversations > 100 cold signups
- Every call = ask for 2 referrals

## Target Personas — Priority Order

### Tier 1: Postdocs (Highest Priority)
**Why:** Active drafts, no institutional purchasing friction, highest pain, most likely to trial immediately, fast feedback loop.

**Profile:**
- 1–4 years post-PhD, active lab affiliation
- Fields: HCI, ML/AI, computational social science, biomedical, public health
- Signal: Recent preprint on arXiv/bioRxiv, lab page lists them, LinkedIn active

**Volume target:** 20 emails Days 1–2, 10 emails Days 3–4

**Message angle:** "try it on one real draft this week, tell me what's broken"

---

### Tier 2: Senior PhD Students (4th–6th Year)
**Why:** High pain (advisor feedback bottleneck is real), digitally adventurous, no institutional purchasing required, fastest to trial.

**Profile:**
- Late-stage PhD, has a chapter or conference paper in progress
- Advisor is someone on your target list (warm second-touch opportunity)
- Fields: same as Tier 1

**Volume target:** 15 emails Days 1–3

**Message angle:** "what if you could get reviewer-style feedback before sending a draft to your advisor?"

---

### Tier 3: Assistant Professors (First 5 Years)
**Why:** Still writing actively, mentoring grad students who are your Tier 1–2 targets, future lab-access buyers, less likely to ignore cold email than senior professors, care about publishing efficiency.

**Profile:**
- Tenure-track, 0–5 years post-hire
- Active publication list (2+ papers last 2 years)
- Visible grad students on lab page
- Fields: HCI, ML, biomedical engineering, CS, psychology, public health

**Volume target:** 15 emails Days 1–2, 10 follow-ups Day 4

**Message angle:** "would this help your students improve drafts before they land in your inbox?"

---

### Tier 4: Associate Professors With Active Labs
**Why:** Lab-access buyers. Already past tenure stress, thinking about lab efficiency. Less urgency to try it personally but very likely to forward to a postdoc or PhD student.

**Profile:**
- Active lab with 5+ grad students or postdocs
- Lab page shows recent publications
- Fields same as above

**Volume target:** 10 emails Days 3–4

**Message angle:** PI framing from prompt.md — lab workflow, not personal use

---

### Tier 5: Research Scientists / Lab Managers
**Why:** Underutilized target. Often the actual person coordinating manuscript submissions. Mid-level role, not PI-gated, have hands-on draft pain. Easy to forget this persona.

**Profile:**
- "Research scientist" or "lab manager" title
- Listed on lab pages at GT/Emory
- Not in the spotlight, will respond to direct practical framing

**Volume target:** 5 emails Days 3–4

## Email Strategy Improvements

### Subject Line Strategy
The prompt.md subjects are fine but generic. For academic cold email, try these instead:

**For postdocs/PhDs:**
- `quick question about [paper title or research area]`
- `would this help before your next submission?`
- `draft feedback before advisor or reviewer sees it`
- `[Field] draft review — free beta`

**For PIs:**
- `draft QA for your lab before submission`
- `question about [lab name] workflow`
- `free beta: reviewer-style critique for your lab`

**A/B rule:** Try 2 subject lines across Day 1–2 sends. Keep the one that gets more opens on Day 3+. If you have no open tracking, use gut feel based on reply rate.

### Timing
- **Best days:** Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday
- **Best time:** 8–10am local time (before their day ramps up) or 4–6pm (end of day scan)
- Avoid Monday (inbox overload) and Friday (mentally off)

### Personalization Formula (3-sentence hook)
Every email must have this before the pitch:

```
I saw [specific thing: paper title / lab focus / recent preprint].
[One sentence connecting that to the draft review pain].
[Product pitch sentence].
```

Without this, reply rate drops sharply. Generic emails = spam folder.

## Response Handling

### Someone replies positively
1. Reply within 2 hours (not 2 days)
2. Book call immediately — offer 3 specific times in next 48h
3. Send beta link before the call, ask them to try one feature if possible
4. On call: use call script from prompt.md

### Someone says "not right now"
1. Ask: "would you know a postdoc or PhD student in your lab who deals more with submission prep?"
2. This turns a rejection into a warm referral. Works often.

### Someone says "cool, I'll check it out"
1. Send beta link with specific instruction: "try uploading a draft + your literature folder, see what it flags"
2. Follow up in 3 days: "did anything feel useful or broken?"
3. No reply after that = move to Follow-Up 2 from prompt.md

### Someone asks about bugs / reliability
Use beta framing from prompt.md verbatim. Do not overpromise.

## Channel Supplement: LinkedIn (5–10 messages/day)

LinkedIn works differently than email for academics. Use it for:
- Researchers where you can't find a direct email
- Warm second touch after someone opens your email (you can check if they're active on LinkedIn)
- Targeting researchers who post about manuscript/review frustrations

**LinkedIn message (short):**
```
Hi [Name], I'm building Noesis — a tool that reviews a draft against your own literature library and flags unsupported claims and coverage gaps before submission. Not AI writing — more like a pre-submission peer reviewer.

Working on beta validation with GT/Emory researchers this week. Would you be open to a 15-minute call or a quick trial?

Demo: [link]
```

## What Not To Do

- Do not send batch emails with [FIRST NAME] merge fails
- Do not pitch lab access before validating individual use
- Do not overpromise polish or stability ("production-ready" = lie, don't say it)
- Do not follow up more than twice total (prompt.md has the right 2-email follow-up cadence)
- Do not send to ultra-famous PIs without a warm intro or hyper-specific hook
- Do not use HTML templates or tracking pixels in Gmail (spam signals)
- Do not write emails longer than 120 words

## What Good Looks Like By End of Day 5
- 80–100 emails sent
- 5–10 replies of any kind
- 3–5 calls booked or completed
- 2–3 beta users who actually tried the product
- 1 clear "I would pay for this" signal
- 2+ referrals to new contacts not in original list

If none of this is happening by Day 3, the subject lines or personalization is the problem. Revise and retest — don't just send more volume.
