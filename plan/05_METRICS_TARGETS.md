## Unit Economics Targets

### Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)

**Free Users:**
- LTV: $0 (indirect value via referrals, word-of-mouth)

**Pro Users:**
- ARPU: $12/month
- Average lifetime: 5 months (20% monthly churn)
- LTV: $12 × 5 = $60

**Team Users:**
- ARPU: $10/user/month ($50 for 5 users)
- Average lifetime: 10 months (10% monthly churn - lower churn due to team lock-in)
- LTV: $10 × 10 = $100

**Enterprise:**
- ARPU: $50/user/year = $4.17/user/month
- Average lifetime: 24 months (5% monthly churn - very low due to contracts)
- LTV: $4.17 × 24 = $100 per user
- Per university: $10-20K/year contract = $240-480K over 2 years

**Blended LTV (Month 6):**
- 60% Pro: $60 LTV
- 30% Team: $100 LTV
- 10% Enterprise: $100 LTV
- **Weighted average:** $75 LTV

### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

**By Channel:**
- Word-of-mouth: $0 CAC
- University partnerships: $10 CAC (marketing materials, events)
- Content marketing: $5 CAC (time investment, tool costs)
- Product Hunt: $0 CAC (one-time effort)
- Paid ads: $50 CAC (Month 4-6 only)

**Blended CAC (Month 6):**
- 40% Word-of-mouth: $0
- 30% University: $10
- 15% Content: $5
- 10% Product Hunt: $0
- 5% Paid ads: $50
- **Weighted average:** ~$10 CAC

### LTV:CAC Ratio

**Month 6:**
- LTV: $75
- CAC: $10
- **Ratio: 7.5:1** (excellent - target was 6:1)

**Benchmark:**
- <1:1 = Losing money
- 1:1 to 3:1 = Breaking even to marginally profitable
- 3:1 to 5:1 = Healthy SaaS business
- >5:1 = Very efficient (but may be under-investing in growth)

**Noesis at 7.5:1 = Room to invest more in paid acquisition while maintaining healthy unit economics**

## Churn Analysis

### Monthly Churn Rate Targets

| Month | Churn Rate | Retention Rate | Notes |
|-------|------------|----------------|-------|
| **Month 1** | 30% | 70% | Expected high churn (early product) |
| **Month 2** | 25% | 75% | Product improvements reduce churn |
| **Month 3** | 20% | 80% | Monetization focuses on engaged users |
| **Month 4-5** | 20% | 80% | Stable churn |
| **Month 6** | 15% | 85% | Enterprise contracts reduce churn |

### Churn Reasons (Expected Distribution)

- **Product not solving problem:** 40% (need better product-market fit)
- **Too expensive:** 20% (pricing sensitivity)
- **Not using regularly:** 25% (activation/engagement issue)
- **Switched to competitor:** 5% (low - no direct competitor)
- **Other:** 10% (life changes, graduation, etc.)

### Churn Reduction Tactics

**Product improvements:**
- Better onboarding (reduce "not using regularly" churn)
- More features (reduce "product not solving problem" churn)
- Performance improvements (reduce frustration churn)

**Pricing:**
- Free tier limits (convert or churn inactive users intentionally)
- Annual plans with discount (lock in for longer)
- Team plans (social lock-in reduces churn)

**Engagement:**
- Email nurture campaigns
- In-app notifications (new features, tips)
- User success calls (proactive outreach to at-risk users)
